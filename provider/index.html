What a placement includes

The same six things every time, whether you book three hours a week or live-in care.

  • A matched caregiverChosen for the language spoken at home and the kind of help needed.
  • A written care planAgreed with the family before the first visit, so nothing is assumed.
  • Check-in and check-outRecorded on every visit, so attendance is never a matter of memory.
  • Visit notes the same dayA short account of how the day went, readable by the family you name.
  • A named coordinatorOne person who knows the case and answers the phone.
  • Cover when neededA relief caregiver who already has the care plan, if yours is unwell.
We provide caregivers — not nurses or doctors. Our team assists with daily living, hygiene, mobility and companionship. Clinical treatment stays with your own doctor or licensed home-nursing provider.

What our caregivers do

Care is arranged by the hour, by the day, or live-in. Every task below is agreed in the care plan before a caregiver starts, so nobody is guessing what is in scope.

Daily personal care

The everyday help that keeps someone comfortable, clean and safe at home.

  • Bathing, showering and sponge baths
  • Adult diaper and pad changes
  • Dressing, grooming and oral care
  • Toileting and continence support
  • Repositioning and pressure-sore prevention
  • Feeding assistance and meal preparation

Mobility & companionship

Support that keeps someone moving, oriented and connected to the day.

  • Transfers, walking and wheelchair support
  • Escort to hospital and clinic appointments
  • Gentle prescribed exercises and stretching
  • Medication reminders at set times
  • Conversation, reading and company
  • Light housekeeping around the person

Massage & wellness

Booked as single sessions or added to an ongoing care schedule.

  • Relaxation and comfort massage
  • Post-natal massage and confinement support
  • Gentle limb massage for bed-bound clients
  • Assisted stretching for stiffness
  • Warm compress and comfort routines
  • Single visits or a standing weekly slot

Need something not listed? Put it in the quote form — if it sits outside what a caregiver may do, we will say so plainly and point you to the right provider.

Care around common conditions

Most families come to us because of a diagnosis, a fall or a hospital discharge. Our caregivers don't treat the condition — your doctor does — but they know what daily life with it looks like, and the care plan is written around it.

Dementia & memory loss

A familiar face and a steady routine. Gentle prompting for meals, washing and dressing, supervision against wandering, and patience with repeated questions.

After a stroke

Safe transfers, help with one-sided weakness, the home exercises your therapist set, and escort to rehab appointments.

Home from hospital

The first weeks after surgery or an admission: help getting up and about, personal care, meals, and someone to notice when something isn't right.

Parkinson's & frailty

Support with walking and balance, fall prevention around the home, and unhurried help with eating, dressing and bathing.

Bed-bound care

Regular repositioning, bed baths, diaper changes, skin checks for pressure areas, and limb massage for comfort.

Palliative & end of life

Comfort, dignity and company alongside the palliative team, and relief for family members who need to rest.

How it works

From first call to first visit is usually three to five working days — faster when it is urgent.

The home assessment is the part families tell us matters most. We meet the person, see the home, and write the care plan with you rather than handing you a standard package.

  1. Tell us the situation

    A short call or the quote form. Who needs care, what they need help with, and when.

  2. Home assessment

    We visit, meet the person and the family, and write the care plan together — tasks, hours, house rules.

  3. Matched caregiver

    We propose a caregiver matched on language, experience and schedule. You meet them before confirming.

  4. Care begins

    Visits start on the agreed schedule. You see check-ins and visit notes, and your coordinator stays reachable.

Questions applicants ask

I have no caregiving experience. Can I still apply?

Yes. Tell us about anything relevant — looking after a family member counts. New caregivers start with simpler cases.

Can I choose my hours?

Yes. Tell us whether you want full time, part time, live-in or weekends, and placements are matched to that.

Where is the work?

In clients' own homes across the Klang Valley. We match you to the area you say you can work in, so travel stays reasonable.

How do I get my schedule?

Through the Sutera caregiver app: your shifts, the care plan for each client, check-in and check-out, and visit notes.

Do you hire nurses?

Not for nursing work. We place caregivers for daily living support, not clinical care. Nurses are welcome to apply as caregivers if that is the work they want.

Questions families ask

Are your caregivers nurses?

No, and we are careful about this. Our caregivers assist with daily living — bathing, changing, feeding, mobility, company and appointment escort. They do not give injections, dress wounds, manage IV lines, insert or change catheters, or make any clinical decision. If your situation needs that, you need a licensed home-nursing provider, and we will tell you so rather than take the booking.

Can a caregiver give medication?

They can remind and prompt at set times, hand over a pre-sorted dose, and record that it was taken. They do not adjust doses or administer anything by injection. Medication should be sorted into a weekly pill organiser by the family or a pharmacist.

What if we do not get on with the caregiver?

Tell your coordinator and we will change the placement. A poor match is a normal thing that happens in home care, and it is better raised in week one than endured.

What happens if our caregiver is ill?

Your coordinator arranges a relief caregiver who already has the care plan. For live-in placements, a named relief caregiver is agreed before care starts.

Do you provide care outside the Klang Valley?

We are starting in the Klang Valley. Elsewhere we take the enquiry and tell you honestly whether we can staff it, rather than promising and failing.

How is the family kept informed?

Caregivers check in and out of every visit and write short visit notes. Family members with access can read them the same day, along with the schedule for the week ahead.

Who can see my family member's information?

Only the assigned caregiver, your coordinator, and the family members you name. Access is logged. We treat health information as sensitive personal data under Malaysia's Personal Data Protection Act 2010.

Is Sutera Care Provider the same as Sutera Cares?

They are related but separate. Sutera Cares is a non-profit medical fund for refugees in Malaysia. Sutera Care Provider is a commercial home-care company. Paying clients here do not fund the charity automatically, and charity donations never subsidise this business — the two keep separate books.
