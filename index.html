Why Sutera Cares exists

≈190,000

refugees and asylum-seekers are registered with UNHCR in Malaysia — with no legal status, no right to work, and no public health coverage.

RM 3,000+

is what a hospital can ask as a deposit before a delivery — often equal to three months of a refugee family's income.

24–48 h

is how fast Sutera Cares moves from an emergency referral to a funding decision — because emergencies need money in hours, not weeks.

At government hospitals, refugees pay "foreigner" rates. A UNHCR card halves the bill, but a single delivery can still cost thousands of ringgit — and unpaid bills bring debt, blacklisting from future care, and fear. Pregnancy is the most common reason a refugee family faces a large medical bill. Emergencies are the most catastrophic. Clinics for refugees exist; a rapid, community-led way to pay the bill and walk the family through it did not. That is the gap we fill.

How a case works

  1. Call

    A family, community leader, clinic or hospital social worker messages our WhatsApp hotline — in Bahasa Malaysia, English, Rohingya, Burmese or Arabic.

  2. Verify

    We check UNHCR documents, medical papers and the hospital's own quote, and confirm with a community leader — within 24 hours for emergencies.

  3. Fund

    The Emergency Fund or a named campaign covers the bill. Payment goes to the hospital or clinic directly — never cash, and every receipt is published.

  4. Accompany

    A navigator sits with the family through admission, treatment and paperwork — including the newborn's birth certificate and UNHCR registration.

  5. Follow up

    Postnatal and recovery checks at day 7 and 6 weeks. The case closes with its outcome, and every donor gets the third update: Recovered.

Who we are

Sutera is Malay for silk — from the Sanskrit sutra, "thread". The thread that holds: soft as silk, strong as a lifeline.

Sutera Cares was founded by a refugee community leader with trust and reach inside refugee communities in Malaysia, together with a Malaysian co-founder with standing in the local community, mosques and institutions — the two halves this problem needs.

The fund is governed by an independent Malaysian committee including doctors, an accountant and a lawyer, while a Community Advisory Council of refugee leaders keeps the work community-led. We operate under a registered Malaysian partner organisation while our own society registration is in progress.

We serve any refugee or asylum-seeker — any ethnicity, any faith — starting in the Klang Valley.

What we deliberately don't do

  • Run a clinic — we fund and navigate care at existing ones.
  • Hand cash to patients — payments go to providers.
  • Duplicate other NGOs — we plug the financing gap and refer the rest.
  • Make loans — help is a gift, never a debt.
  • Publish anyone's face, name or location without consent.

Questions donors ask us

Why refugees, when Malaysians also need help?

Malaysians in hardship have government schemes and subsidised care; refugees have none of it. Kasih is not zero-sum — many of our donors also give to Malaysian causes, and we cheer them on.

Is this legal?

Yes. Donations are received by a registered organisation — our Malaysian partner now, our registered society once approved — and paid directly to hospitals and clinics.

Is my donation tax-deductible?

Not yet. Tax-exempt status in Malaysia takes about two years of audited accounts. Where a gift can be routed through a partner whose approval covers it, we will say so clearly.

Where does my money go?

100% of campaign gifts go to patients; operations are funded by Sutera Circle monthly donors. Every case appears on the public ledger with receipts.

How do you know a case is real?

UNHCR documents, medical papers, the hospital's own quote, verification by a community leader — and we confirm with the hospital before any payment is made.

What if you raise more than a case needs?

The surplus rolls into the Emergency Fund for the next family. This sentence appears on every campaign page.

Can I give zakat?

Yes — to the ring-fenced zakat pool for eligible beneficiaries. General funds serve every refugee, of every faith.

Can my company be involved?

Yes: Safe Birth Sponsor packages (RM 10,000 sponsors five births, with a named outcomes report), mother-and-baby kit days, and volunteer navigators from your team.
